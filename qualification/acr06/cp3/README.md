# ACR-06 CP3: where ScanWeave's remaining bytes go, and one compression hypothesis

**Baseline:** the current default output (main d804bb0 / 261c27f, `--profile clearscan --glyph-sharing off`) on the fixed 32-page group corpus: financial 13, forms 2, historical 5, mixed 2, receipts 10.

**Comparators:** the frozen arms in `pdf_packaging/groups/`:
- Adobe XI ClearScan;
- Archive MRC;
- OCRmyPDF -O3 and -O1.

**CamScanner:** its web output is raster-only (one JPEG per page, no text layer; see `../cp2/`). It's a document-cleanup comparator for 4 matched real pages, not a reconstruction competitor. On those pages it is larger than ScanWeave on 3 of 4 and has no search (`../cp2/arm-accounting.json`).

## Ranked: where ScanWeave's bytes go

1. **Tonal page backgrounds that still carry raster text: 5.48 MB** of 200-DPI Q85 JPEG on 28 pages, 67% of the corpus.
   - **Why it's large:** ScanWeave draws only part of the text as vectors. Text ink drawn as vectors: financial 47.7%, mixed 31.1%, receipts 23.6%, forms 11.7%, historical 0.1%. The rest stays in the background.
   - **Adobe:** draws nearly all its text visibly as font glyphs. Its financial backgrounds are about 5 KB per page at 150 DPI, against about 152 KB for ScanWeave.
   - **Counterfactual re-encodings of ScanWeave's own backgrounds** (`background_counterfactuals.py`; PIL calibration 5.11 MB against 5.53 MB stored):

     | Change | Bytes | Change vs calibration |
     |---|---:|---:|
     | 150 DPI | 3.25 MB | −36% |
     | Q75 | 4.37 MB | −15% |
     | Residual ink removed | 4.21 MB | −18% |
     | Paper within ±12 flattened | 4.40 MB | −14% |

   - **Resolution is the largest single factor.** Neither leftover ink nor paper grain alone explains the size.
2. **Fonts: 1.08 MB.** Financial alone is 750 KB, against Adobe's 367 KB, while ScanWeave vectorises less of that text. This is outline count, not compression; it belongs to the q3 / C2 track (#27).
3. **Lossless 8-bit Flate pages: 0.85 MB**, mainly forms, 738 KB on 2 pages. The pages are already grayscale with all 256 tones, so a lossless palette gives no gain. Whether they're eligible for JPEG is governed by the ACR-04/05 rules.
4. **Content streams: 0.32 MB**, against Adobe's 0.11 MB. ScanWeave writes one text matrix per glyph and repeats the `Tf`/`Tr` state. Slimming that is a small, low-risk saving.
5. **Raster patches 0.32 MB; structure 0.10 MB.**

## Separating raster text from the paper (an MRC-style counterfactual)

`split_counterfactual.py` takes ink darker than local paper by more than 40 as a 1-bit Flate mask at 300 DPI. The ink-free paper is encoded as JPEG at 150 or 100 DPI.

- **Bytes:** 5.53 MB becomes 2.92 MB (paper at 150 DPI) or 1.61 MB (paper at 100 DPI). The mask is 0.58 MB.
- **Appearance:** the panels (`panels.py`) show it is visibly destructive. Faint, tonal historical ink is only partly captured and turns into black blotches, and printed type becomes blocky.
- **Archive MRC,** which does this at scale, is visibly softer on historical and receipt text.

An MRC split would need a tonal foreground model. That is the broad rewrite the mission excludes without evidence, and this evidence argues against the naive form.

## Selected hypothesis: one bounded experiment (not implemented; needs owner approval)

**Encode eligible whole-page tonal residual backgrounds at about 150 DPI instead of about 200 DPI.** Keep the existing ACR-04 eligibility, quality (Q85 4:2:0), complete-PDF size selection and the faithful profile unchanged.

- **Expected:** about −36% of background bytes, roughly −1.9 MB or −23% of the corpus (8.18 MB to about 6.2 MB). Adobe is at 4.18 MB. The remaining gap is vector coverage, which is out of scope.
- **Raster-text legibility proxy** (`legibility.py`, Tesseract on the background against the source's words, 33 backgrounds): 1,499 words recovered now, 1,485 at 150 DPI (−0.9%).
- **Panels:** visually indistinguishable at reading size on historical, receipt and financial raster text.
- **Qualification it would need:**
  - an opt-in flag first;
  - the full corpus plus the fresh sets, with visual review of every page's raster text at 300 DPI;
  - render fidelity against the source;
  - the text layer and search checked unchanged;
  - no change to the default without owner approval.
- **Risks:** finer raster text (small receipt print, Fraktur) softens. ACR-04 chose 200 DPI on its own evidence, so this is a reviewed change to that parameter, not a new codec.
